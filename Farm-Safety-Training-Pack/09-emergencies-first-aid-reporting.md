# Module 09 — Emergencies, First Aid & Reporting

*When something goes wrong, the first few minutes matter most. Know this before you need it.*

---

## In an emergency — the order of actions

1. **Stop the danger if you safely can.** Safe Stop any machine. Don't rush into danger and become a second casualty — that's how rescuers get killed (slurry gas, electricity, machinery).
2. **Make the area safe** — switch off power/PTO, keep others back.
3. **Check the casualty** — are they responsive? Breathing?
4. **Call 999** (or 112). Ask for **Ambulance** (and Fire/Police if needed).
5. **Give first aid** within your training. Don't move someone with a possible spinal injury unless they're in immediate danger.
6. **Send someone to meet the ambulance** at the road/gate to guide them in — farms are hard to find.

## Common farm injuries — quick first aid pointers

*(These are pointers only — a proper first aid course is strongly recommended. The farm should have a trained first aider and a stocked kit.)*

- **Severe bleeding:** press hard directly on the wound with a clean pad, keep pressing, raise the limb if you can, call 999. Don't remove a soaked pad — add more on top.
- **Crush / entanglement / amputation:** call 999 immediately. Control bleeding. **Keep any severed part** — wrap in clean material, put in a bag, keep cool (not directly on ice) and send with the casualty.
- **Broken bones:** don't move unnecessarily; support the limb; 999.
- **Unconscious but breathing:** put in the **recovery position**, monitor breathing, 999.
- **Not breathing:** start **CPR** if trained; 999 will talk you through it; use a defibrillator if available.
- **Chemical in eyes/skin:** flush with lots of clean water for at least 10–15 minutes; take the product label/safety data sheet to hospital.
- **Suspected slurry gas / electric shock:** **do not approach** until the area is confirmed safe — call 999 and keep clear.
- **Heatstroke:** move to shade, cool rapidly, 999 if confused/collapsed.

## Reporting — accidents AND near misses

- **Report every accident, injury and near miss** to your supervisor straight away — even if no one was hurt. A near miss is a free warning; reporting it stops the next one being a tragedy.
- The farm should record incidents in an **accident book**.
- Some incidents must be reported to HSE by the **employer** under **RIDDOR** (Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations) — e.g. deaths, specified serious injuries, injuries causing more than 7 days off work, and certain dangerous occurrences. **That's the employer's duty — your job is to tell your supervisor everything.**
- Never hide a mistake or a near miss out of embarrassment. Speaking up is exactly what a good worker does.
