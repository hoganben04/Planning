# Module 04 — Telehandlers & Loaders

*Telehandlers are involved in serious crush, overturn, falling-load and overhead-line deaths every year. This is high-risk work — treat the training seriously.*

---

## The law and training — read this first

- A telehandler is a **rough-terrain lift truck**. Lift trucks are covered by the HSE **Approved Code of Practice "Rideable lift trucks"** and by **PUWER** and **LOLER**.
- **Anyone under 16 must never operate a telehandler.**
- A **young person (under 18)** must **not** operate one unless they have the **necessary competence and maturity** *and* have completed **appropriate, accredited training** *and* are **adequately supervised**.
- **On a public road**, telehandlers are treated as agricultural vehicles needing a car (Category B) licence — possible from 17, but in practice most operators are **21+** for road and insurance reasons. **Assume road use is not for a 16-year-old.**

> **Action for Truleigh Manor:** book **accredited operator training (Lantra or NPTC)** before the trainee uses the telehandler. This pack prepares them for it and reinforces it — it does not replace it. Early use must be **directly supervised**.

---

## Why telehandlers are dangerous

- They are **heavy and can tip** — sideways or forwards — especially with a raised, extended or heavy load.
- The **boom reaches up and out**, so it can hit **overhead power lines** and unbalance the machine.
- **Loads can fall** — bales especially — onto people nearby.
- People get **crushed** between the machine and walls/gates, or struck while it reverses.
- Using one as a **work platform** (lifting people on the forks or in an unapproved cage) has killed people — **never lift a person except in a proper, tested man-basket designed and rated for that telehandler**.

---

## Pre-use checks

- [ ] Tyres, condition and pressure.
- [ ] Hydraulic hoses and rams — no leaks, no damage.
- [ ] Boom, forks/attachment, and the **quick-hitch locked and pinned**.
- [ ] Load chart / **rated capacity indicator** present and working.
- [ ] Brakes, steering, lights, beacon, horn, reversing alarm, mirrors/camera.
- [ ] Seatbelt — **wear it**.
- [ ] Cab clean, controls understood, nothing loose.
- [ ] Attachment correct for the job (bale spike/grab for round bales — **not the bucket**).

---

## Stability — the rules that keep it upright

- **Know the load chart.** Capacity drops sharply as you raise and extend the boom. Stay within it — never guess.
- **Travel with the load LOW** and the boom retracted, tilted back. A raised load while driving is how telehandlers tip.
- **Travel and turn slowly.** Most overturns happen turning with a raised load or on a slope.
- On slopes: keep the **load on the uphill side**, travel up/down rather than across, and keep the load low.
- Use the machine on **firm, level ground** for lifting. Soft or uneven ground can let it tip.
- **Don't exceed the reach** — extending forward shifts the centre of gravity past the front wheels.
- If fitted, watch the **longitudinal stability indicator** and stop before it warns.

---

## Loading, lifting and placing

- Approach the load square, forks level, at the right width.
- Lift smoothly; tilt back slightly to cradle the load.
- Make sure the load is **stable and won't roll/slide** before you move.
- Keep **people well clear** — nobody under a raised load or in the swing/travel path, ever.
- Place loads on **firm, level ground** and build stacks square (see Module 06 for bale stacking).
- Lower the boom fully and tilt forks down to release; reverse out straight.

## Overhead power lines — a telehandler special

The raised boom can reach lines you'd never touch with a tractor. **Electricity can jump (arc) to the boom without touching it.**

- Know where every overhead line on the farm runs (Module 08).
- **Never raise the boom near lines.** Plan stacking and tipping away from them.
- If you ever contact a line: **stay in the cab**, lower/reverse clear if you can, and **don't let anyone touch the machine**. If you must get out (e.g. fire), **jump clear with both feet together and don't touch machine and ground at the same time**, then shuffle away.

---

## Getting on and off / parking

- Three points of contact, face the machine, never jump.
- Park on level ground, **forks/attachment flat on the ground**, boom retracted, Safe Stop (brake, neutral, off, key out).
- Never leave it with the boom raised or a load up.

---

## Discussion / demonstration (then accredited training)

- Read the **load chart** and explain how capacity changes as the boom raises and extends.
- Explain why you travel with the load low.
- Show where the overhead lines are and how you'd plan a stacking job to avoid them.
- Demonstrate (supervised) picking up, carrying low, and placing a round bale with the correct attachment.
