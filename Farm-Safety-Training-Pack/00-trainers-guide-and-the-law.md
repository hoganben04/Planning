# Module 00 — Trainer's Guide & The Law

*For the employer / supervisor. Read this before you start the trainee on the pack.*

---

## 1. Your legal duties as the employer

If a young person works on your farm — paid, unpaid, family member or not — you have legal duties under:

- **Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974** — the general duty to ensure, so far as reasonably practicable, the health, safety and welfare of workers.
- **Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999, regulation 19** — the specific duty regarding **young persons (under 18)**.
- **Prevention of Accidents to Children in Agriculture Regulations 1998** — age limits for riding on/driving machines.
- **Provision and Use of Work Equipment Regulations 1998 (PUWER)** — equipment must be suitable, maintained, guarded, and only used by people who have had adequate training.
- **Lifting Operations and Lifting Equipment Regulations 1998 (LOLER)** — for lifting (telehandler forks, bale grabs, etc.).

### The young-person rule that matters most
Before the young person starts work you **must** carry out (and act on) a **risk assessment that specifically takes account of**:

- their **inexperience**, **immaturity** and **lack of awareness** of risks;
- the work, equipment and substances involved;
- the layout of the workplace;
- the extent of health and safety training to be provided.

A blank template is provided in **Module 12** — completing it is not optional.

## 2. Age rules — what a person of each age may legally do

> These are legal *minimums*. Being old enough is not the same as being competent. Competence = training + supervision + demonstrated ability. The width/road rules below apply to **public roads**; on private land the duty is to be trained, competent and adequately supervised.

| Activity | Under 13 | 13–15 | 16 | 17 | 18+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ride on / drive a tractor or self-propelled machine | **No (illegal)** | Drive on private land **only after certified training** | Yes (trained) | Yes | Yes |
| Drive a tractor on a **public road** | No | No | Yes — **Category F licence**; tractor **≤ 2.45 m wide**, trailer ≤ 2.45 m wide (2 wheels, or 4 close-coupled) | Yes — width limits lifted | Yes |
| Operate **balers, harvesters, mowers, tedders, PTO-driven machines, processors** | **No (illegal)** | **No (illegal)** | Yes, **if trained, competent and supervised** | Yes | Yes |
| Operate a **lift truck / telehandler** | **No (illegal)** | **No (illegal)** | **Private land only**, and **never** without training, competence, maturity **and** adequate supervision | As 16, plus may take the test for road use | Yes (trained/certified) |
| Drive a telehandler **on a public road** | No | No | No | Cat B test possible at 17; in practice usually **21** for insurance/road use | Yes |
| Use **ATV / quad bike** | **No (illegal)** | **No (illegal)** | Yes, with appropriate training | Yes | Yes |
| Apply **pesticides** | No | No | Generally **16+** with a recognised certificate of competence | Yes | Yes |

**Bottom line for a 16-year-old at Truleigh Manor:** they may legally drive tractors and operate field machinery (tedders, mowers, balers, wrappers) and a telehandler on the farm's own land **provided they are properly trained, demonstrably competent, and supervised to a degree that matches their experience.** Telehandler and ATV use should be backed by **accredited (Lantra/NPTC) training**.

## 3. The level of supervision

Supervision is not "in the same field somewhere." For a young, inexperienced worker it means, in the early stages:

- **Direct supervision** (supervisor present and watching) for any new machine or new task;
- progressing to **general supervision** (checked frequently, can summon help) only once competence is signed off;
- **never** lone working on high-risk tasks until experienced — and arguably not for telehandler/bale-stacking work for some time.

Match the supervision to the task and the person, and **write down** in the risk assessment what level applies to what.

## 4. How to run this pack

1. **Complete Module 12 (risk assessment) first** — before any work.
2. Do **Module 01 (induction)** on day one, on site, walking the actual farm.
3. Work through **Modules 02–09** as classroom/yard sessions mixed with practical demonstration. Don't rush — a module a session is fine.
4. For each machine, use **Module 13** to demonstrate, observe, and sign off competence **before** unsupervised use.
5. Have the trainee complete the **Module 10 quiz**; mark it with **Module 11**. Aim for 100% on the safety-critical questions (Safe Stop, PTO, telehandler stability, overhead lines, emergencies) — re-teach anything missed.
6. Complete **Module 14 (training record & certificate)** and keep the whole pack on file.
7. **Refresh** key points regularly — a 5-minute toolbox talk at the start of busy days.

---

## 5. Records to keep on file

- Completed Module 12 risk assessment (reviewed if anything changes).
- Completed Module 13 competency sign-offs.
- Marked Module 10 quiz.
- Module 14 training record + certificate.
- Copies of any external certificates (Lantra/NPTC telehandler, ATV, etc.).
- Employer's Liability insurance certificate (check young/family workers are covered).

These records are what you produce if HSE ever asks, or if there is an incident.
