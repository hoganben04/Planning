# Module 06 — Hay & Haylage Operations (Field to Stack)

*Putting it all together: the actual job you'll be doing this summer, and the specific risks at each stage.*

---

## What we're making, and why it changes the risks

- **Hay** is grass cut, dried in the field (turned/tedded over several days), then baled dry. Risks: long hot days, dust, fire, machinery, bale handling.
- **Haylage** is grass cut, wilted for a shorter time, baled at higher moisture, then **wrapped airtight in plastic film** so it ferments. Extra risks: the **wrapper**, **heavier wetter bales**, and handling/stacking **film-wrapped** bales that can roll.

Both involve the same chain: **mow → ted/turn → row up (rake) → bale → (wrap, for haylage) → cart → stack**. Every stage has a tractor and a PTO machine, and every stage ends with **heavy bales that must be moved and stacked**.

---

## Stage-by-stage safety

### 1. Mowing
- Pre-start checks (Module 03), sharp undamaged blades.
- **Walk/check the field** for hazards first: people, animals, ground nesting, debris, hidden ditches, soft ground, slopes.
- Keep bystanders **well clear** — mowers throw stones and blade fragments.
- Mind the slopes (Module 03) — drive up/down, slow, no sharp turns.

### 2. Tedding & raking
- Watch folding wings (crush points) and flung debris (eye protection).
- Wait for tines to **stop** before clearing tangled grass.

### 3. Baling
- Tall machine, **high centre of gravity** — slow on slopes, avoid holes/drop-offs.
- **Never** clear the pick-up or chamber while running (Module 05).
- **Stand clear of the rear** when a round bale is ejected.

### 4. Wrapping (haylage)
- Stay **outside the rotating arm/ring radius**.
- Reload film with the machine **stopped**.
- Haylage bales are **heavier** (more moisture) — factor that into telehandler load limits.

### 5. Carting & stacking — where bales fall on people
This stage causes many of the "struck by / falling object" deaths.

- Move bales with the **right attachment** (bale spike or grab) on the telehandler — **never the loader bucket**, which lets bales roll back toward the cab/operator.
- Carry bales **low**; travel and turn slowly (Module 04).
- **No one walks or stands near the telehandler** while it's working or in the stacking area. Falling and rolling bales kill.
- Don't overload trailers; secure loads; **no riding on the load**.

---

## Stacking bales safely

- Build stacks on **firm, level ground**, square and stable, **bonded** (overlapping) like brickwork so they don't topple.
- **Don't stack too high** for the base — tall narrow stacks collapse. Follow the farm's agreed maximum.
- Keep stacks **away from overhead power lines**, buildings you don't want to risk, and field edges/slopes.
- **Never climb on a bale stack.** People fall from stacks and are crushed by collapsing stacks. If you must reach height, use proper access — not the stack itself.
- When taking a stack down, work from the **top down**, never pull from the bottom (the classic fatal collapse).
- Round bales **roll** — chock them or stack so they can't run away, especially on any slope. A loose round bale can crush someone.

---

## Fire & spontaneous heating — a real hay risk

Hay baled **too damp** can heat up internally and **spontaneously catch fire** days or weeks later. Dry, dusty conditions plus hot machinery also start field fires.

- Bale hay at the **right moisture** — your supervisor will check.
- **Monitor stacked hay** for heating in the first weeks: a hot, "caramel/burning" smell, steam, or a stack that's hot to a probe is a danger sign — tell your supervisor immediately and **do not** disturb a hot stack (letting air in can cause it to burst into flame).
- Keep a **fire extinguisher on the tractor/baler** and know how to use it.
- Carry a **phone** to call 999. Know the farm's location to give them (Module 09).
- Clear chaff/dust build-up off hot machine parts (manifold, brakes) during long baling days.
- No smoking anywhere near hay, fuel or machinery.

---

## Long days, heat and fatigue

Haymaking means **long hours racing the weather**. That's exactly when accidents spike.

- Drink water regularly; you sweat more than you think in a hot cab.
- Use **sun protection** (hat, sunscreen, cover up) — see Module 07.
- Take **breaks**. Eat. Tiredness slows reactions and judgement.
- **"One more load" in the dark while exhausted** is when people get hurt. If you're too tired, say so. The weather is not worth your safety.
