# Module 08 — Site Hazards (Height, Electricity, Fire, Slurry, Livestock, Chemicals)

*The wider farm hazards beyond the machine you're driving. Even if you're "just" haymaking, you share the site with all of these.*

---

## Working at height

Falls — from machines, trailers, stacks, ladders and fragile roofs — are a major cause of farm deaths.

- **Avoid working at height** where you can. Don't climb on bale stacks, trailers or machines to reach things.
- Use a **proper ladder or platform**, secured, on firm ground, three points of contact.
- **Fragile roofs (asbestos cement, fibre-cement, rooflights) kill** — people fall straight through. **Never walk on a barn roof.** It's a job for specialists with proper equipment.
- Never use telehandler forks as a platform or to lift a person except in a **purpose-made, tested cage**.

---

## Electricity & overhead power lines

A telehandler boom, tipping trailer, or a high load can hit overhead lines — and **electricity can arc across a gap without contact**. This is one of the most preventable farm deaths.

- **Map every overhead line on the farm** and know the safe working clearances. Mark them at the induction (Module 01).
- Plan stacking, tipping and high-reach work **well away** from lines.
- Watch for **underground cables** when digging.
- **If a machine contacts a line:**
  - **Stay in the cab** — you're safest there.
  - If you can, **drive clear** of the line.
  - **Warn everyone to keep back** and not touch the machine.
  - Call the emergency number on the nearest pole / 105 (UK power network) and 999.
  - Only if there's fire and you *must* get out: **jump clear with feet together, don't touch machine and ground at once**, then **shuffle/bunny-hop** away with small steps (the ground itself is live in a gradient).
- Damaged fallen lines: assume **live**, keep everyone **well back**, call for help.

## Fire

- Know where **extinguishers** are (buildings and machines) and how to use them (pull pin, aim at base, sweep).
- Biggest farm fire risks: machinery (hot parts + dust/chaff), fuel, **hay spontaneously heating** (Module 06), and electrical faults.
- Keep dust/chaff cleared off hot machine parts on long days.
- No smoking near fuel, hay or machinery.
- If fire breaks out: get clear, raise the alarm, call **999**, only fight a small fire if safe and you have an escape route behind you. **People before property.**

---

## Slurry, silage effluent & confined spaces

Even on a hay/haylage farm there may be slurry stores, tanks, pits or grain stores.

- **Slurry gas** (especially hydrogen sulphide, released when slurry is mixed/agitated) can **kill in seconds** and has killed whole families. **Stay away from slurry stores during mixing**; keep clear of the area.
- **Confined spaces** (tanks, pits, silos, augers, grain bins) can have **no oxygen or toxic gas**, and grain/material can **engulf and suffocate**. **Never enter a confined space** — it is a specialist job with permits, gas testing and rescue plans. This is **not** a job for a young, inexperienced worker.

---

## Livestock

Cattle are a leading cause of farm deaths — they crush, trample and charge, especially **cows with calves** and **bulls**.

- Don't enter fields/pens with cattle unless told to, and never alone if you're inexperienced.
- Keep an **escape route**; stay near the edge, never get cornered.
- Move calmly and quietly; never get between a cow and her calf.
- Treat **every bull as dangerous**.
- Watch out when crossing fields with stock during haymaking — close gates, keep stock and machinery apart.

---

## Chemicals & fuel

- **Pesticides** require training and certification (16+ with a recognised certificate). Don't handle or apply them without it.
- Diesel, oils, AdBlue, two-stroke: store correctly, avoid skin contact, wash hands before eating, no eating/drinking around chemicals.
- Read labels and **safety data sheets**; wear the PPE the label specifies.
- Know where the **eye wash / first aid** is for splashes (Module 09).

## Discussion / demonstration

- Point out (or mark on a map) every overhead power line on the farm.
- Say exactly what you'd do if the telehandler touched a power line.
- Explain why you must never walk on an old barn roof.
- Explain why you never enter a slurry tank or grain store.
- Describe how to behave around cattle.
