# Module 07 — Manual Handling, PPE, Noise, Dust, Vibration & Heat

*The injuries that build up slowly — your back, your hearing, your lungs, your hands — matter just as much as the dramatic ones.*

---

## Manual handling — protect your back

Farm work means lifting, carrying and dragging. Your body at 16 is still developing — bad lifting now can cause problems for life.

**Before you lift, think:**
- **Can a machine do it?** Use the telehandler/loader for anything heavy. Don't lift what a machine should.
- **Is it too heavy or awkward?** Get help, split the load, or use an aid.
- **Clear the route** — no trip hazards, know where you're putting it down.

**Good lifting technique:**
- Feet apart, stable base, close to the load.
- **Bend your knees, not your back** — keep the back's natural curve.
- Get a secure grip; lift smoothly with your legs.
- Keep the load **close to your body**.
- **Don't twist** while lifting — turn with your feet.
- Put it down the same way — knees, not back.

**On the farm specifically:** twine bales, fence posts, feed bags, drums, machine parts. If you're straining, stop — that's the warning, not a challenge.

## PPE — what to wear and when

PPE is the **last line of defence**, but it matters. Your supervisor provides it; you wear it.

| PPE | When |
|---|---|
| **Safety boots** (steel toe, good grip) | **Always**, every day |
| **Ear protection** (plugs or muffs) | Noisy machines, long cab time, anything where you'd have to raise your voice |
| **Dust mask** (FFP2/FFP3) | Mouldy/dusty hay, dusty cab work, sweeping |
| **Eye protection** | Tedding/mowing (flung debris), grinding, anything that could flick into your eyes |
| **Gloves** | Handling twine/wire, rough materials, chemicals (correct type) |
| **Hi-vis** | Around moving vehicles, road work, poor light |
| **Sun protection** | Long days in the open (see below) |
| **Hard hat** | Where there's a risk of falling objects |

Loose clothing is a hazard around machinery — keep it **fitted and tucked in** (Module 05).

## Noise — you only get one set of ears

Tractors, balers and PTO machines are loud, and **hearing loss is permanent and painless** as it happens — you won't notice until it's gone.

- If you have to **raise your voice** to be heard at arm's length, it's loud enough to need ear protection.
- Wear plugs or muffs and **keep them in** — taking them out "just for a minute" repeatedly defeats them.
- Keep cab doors/windows closed where practical.

---

## Dust & mould — protect your lungs

Hay and haylage are dusty, and mouldy hay carries spores that cause **"Farmer's Lung"** and other serious, sometimes permanent, lung disease.

- Wear an **FFP3 dust mask** when handling dusty or mouldy hay, or working in dust.
- Work **upwind** of dust where you can; keep cab filters in good order and windows shut in heavy dust.
- Don't disturb visibly mouldy bales without a mask.

---

## Vibration — HAVS and whole-body

- **Hand-Arm Vibration (HAVS):** from vibrating tools — causes painful, permanent nerve and circulation damage ("white finger"). Limit time on vibrating tools; keep hands warm; report tingling/numbness early.
- **Whole-body vibration:** from bouncing around in the cab on rough ground all day causes back problems. Keep the **seat suspension set for your weight**, drive at sensible speed for the ground, and take breaks.

---

## Heat, sun & dehydration

Summer haymaking = long hours in the sun and hot cabs.

- **Drink water often** — don't wait until you're thirsty. Keep a bottle in the cab.
- Cover up, wear a hat, use **sunscreen** on exposed skin (skin cancer is a real long-term farm risk).
- Recognise **heat exhaustion**: headache, dizziness, feeling sick, cramps, heavy sweating then clammy/pale — get into shade, cool down, drink, tell someone. Heatstroke (confusion, hot dry skin, collapse) is a **999 emergency**.
- Take breaks in the shade.

## Discussion / demonstration

- Demonstrate a correct lift (knees not back, load close, no twist).
- Say when you'd put on ear protection, a dust mask, and eye protection.
- Explain what Farmer's Lung is and how you avoid it.
- Describe the early signs of heat exhaustion.

**Sign-off:** Trainee can lift safely and knows what PPE to use when, and the health risks PPE protects against.
