# Module 03 — Tractors

*Tractors are the most-used machine on the farm and involved in the most deaths. Respect them.*

---

## Before you drive: the law for a 16-year-old

- On **private farmland**: you may drive a tractor at 16 once you are **trained and competent**, and while you are **supervised** to a level that matches your experience.
- On a **public road**: you need a **Category F licence** (you can get this at 16). At 16 you may only drive a tractor **up to 2.45 m wide**, towing a trailer up to 2.45 m wide (two wheels, or four close-coupled wheels). The width restriction lifts at 17.
- Always wear the **seatbelt** if one is fitted — it keeps you within the protective cab/frame (ROPS) if the tractor overturns.

---

## Daily pre-start checks ("POWER" + walk-round)

Walk around the tractor before you start. A quick check catches most problems:

- [ ] **Tyres** — condition and pressure, no big cuts, wheel nuts present.
- [ ] **Fuel & fluids** — diesel, engine oil, coolant, hydraulic oil; look for leaks under the tractor.
- [ ] **Lights, beacon, indicators, brake lights, mirrors** — working and clean.
- [ ] **Guards** — PTO shaft guard and all guards present and secure.
- [ ] **Cab** — clean glass, working wipers, nothing loose rolling around the floor, fire extinguisher present.
- [ ] **Steps and handholds** — clean of mud so you don't slip.
- [ ] **Linkage / drawbar / pickup hitch** — sound, pins and clips in place.
- [ ] **Brakes** — including that the **brake pedals are latched together** for road work.
- [ ] **Seatbelt** — present and working.
- [ ] **Controls** — know where everything is *before* you move.

Report anything wrong to your supervisor. Don't use a defective tractor.

---

## Getting on and off — the right way

- Use the **steps and handholds**, always **three points of contact** (two hands + one foot, or two feet + one hand).
- Face the tractor when climbing down. **Never jump down** — twisted ankles, slips and falls are common.
- Keep steps clean of mud.
- **Never get on or off a moving tractor.**

---

## Driving safely

- **Seatbelt on, doors closed.**
- Start only from the **driver's seat**, in neutral, PTO off. Never start by bridging the starter from the ground — people are crushed every year by tractors starting in gear.
- Keep speed **sensible for the conditions** — wet grass, slopes, loads and the yard all need slowing down.
- Watch your **width and length**, especially with mounted/towed kit and in gateways.
- **No passengers** unless there's a proper instructor seat with a seatbelt.
- Take corners slowly; a raised load or full trailer changes how the tractor handles.
- In the yard, assume someone could step out — go slow, use the beacon, sound the horn at blind corners.

---

## Slopes and overturning — a top killer

Tractors **overturn sideways** and **roll backwards**. Both kill.

- Know the ground. **Drive up and down slopes, not across**, where possible.
- Slow right down on slopes; **never change direction sharply** on a slope.
- Be very careful near **ditches, banks and soft edges** — a wheel dropping in can flip the tractor.
- Keep loaders and mounted implements **low** when moving — a high load raises the centre of gravity and makes tipping far easier.
- Pulling a stuck load: hitch only to the **drawbar/proper hitch point at the correct height** — never high up. Hitching too high causes the tractor to rear up and flip over backwards.
- If the tractor starts to tip and you have a cab/ROPS and seatbelt: **stay in the seat, hold on, lean away from the direction of fall.** Jumping out usually means being crushed under it.

---

## Trailers

- Match trailer and load to the tractor; don't overload.
- Make sure the **brakes and lights** on the trailer work.
- Check the **hitch is locked** and the safety pin/clip is in.
- Connect the **brake line and breakaway/safety chain**.
- Never stand between tractor and trailer while hitching with the engine running.
- Load evenly and within limits; secure loose loads.
- **Never let anyone ride in or on a trailer.**
- When tipping: tip only on **firm, level ground**, watch for **overhead power lines**, and **never go under a raised body**.

---

## Discussion / demonstration

- Carry out a full pre-start walk-round and tell your supervisor what you're checking and why.
- Mount and dismount correctly (three points of contact).
- Explain why you drive up/down a slope rather than across, and why you never hitch high.
- Demonstrate hitching a trailer and connecting brakes/lights/safety chain.

**Sign-off:** Trainee can safely check, mount, drive, and hitch a tractor under supervision.
