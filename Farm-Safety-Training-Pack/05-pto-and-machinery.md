# Module 05 — PTO & Field Machinery (Tedders, Mowers, Balers, Wrappers)

*This module covers the spinning, cutting, gathering machines used to make hay and haylage. They are unforgiving. The rule is: stop everything before you go near it.*

---

## The PTO shaft — treat it like a live danger

The **Power Take-Off (PTO)** shaft transfers power from the tractor to the machine. It spins at high speed (typically 540 or 1000 rpm). People are **entangled and killed or dismembered** by PTO shafts every year — usually after their clothing catches.

- The PTO shaft **must have its full guard** fitted, in good condition, and the **anti-rotation chains** attached at both ends so the guard doesn't spin. **Never run a machine with a damaged or missing PTO guard.**
- **Never step over, lean over, or reach across** a turning PTO shaft.
- **Tie up loose clothing.** No dangling cords, scarves, untucked shirts, loose sleeves. No jewellery. Long hair tied up and tucked away. A loose drawstring is all it takes.
- **Disengage the PTO and Safe Stop before connecting/disconnecting** the shaft or going anywhere near it.
- Connect the PTO with the **engine off**; make sure it's locked on at both ends.

---

## Guards — never defeat them

Every guard on every machine is there because someone was hurt without it.

- Guards must be **in place and secure** before the machine runs.
- **Never remove, tie back, or wedge open a guard** to make a job easier.
- If a guard is **missing or broken, take the machine out of use** and tell your supervisor.
- Replace guards **before** restarting after any maintenance.

---

## Clearing blockages — the rule that saves hands and lives

Blockages are the **most common reason people reach into running machines** — and one of the most common ways they're killed.

**The procedure, every time:**
1. **Safe Stop** — handbrake, neutral, PTO **disengaged**, engine **off**, key **out and in your pocket**.
2. **Wait** until **all** moving parts have **completely stopped** — flywheels, rotors and tines coast on for several seconds.
3. Only then clear the blockage, using a **tool, not your hands** where possible.
4. Watch for **stored energy** — tensioned springs, raised parts, hydraulic pressure, a pick-up reel under load can spring back. Release/secure it safely.
5. Replace any guard, stand clear, then restart.

**Never** clear a blockage with the engine running. **Never** ask, or let anyone, hold/feed material while the machine runs.

## Tedders & rakes

- Fast-spinning tines fling debris — keep people clear and wear **eye protection**.
- Tines are sharp and the rotors carry momentum — **wait for them to stop** before touching.
- Fold/unfold the wings exactly as the manual says; keep clear of the folding path (crush points).
- Watch width on the road and through gateways.

## Mowers (disc/drum mowers, conditioners)

- Blades are razor-sharp and spin extremely fast; they can **throw stones and broken blade pieces** a long way — keep bystanders well back and never stand in front.
- Check blades/skids before use; replace damaged blades as a set.
- The mower **coasts** after the PTO stops — wait fully before approaching.
- Engage the **breakback/safety device** correctly when transporting.

## Balers (round and conventional)

- The **pick-up, rollers, knotter/wrapping mechanism and chamber** are all entanglement and crush hazards. **Never** reach into the pick-up or chamber while anything moves.
- **Bale ejection:** the tailgate and ejected bale can strike/crush. **Stand clear of the rear** when ejecting; make sure no one is behind.
- On slopes, balers are tall with a **high centre of gravity** — go slow, avoid holes and drop-offs, never turn sharply across a slope.
- Twine/net wrap: thread it with the machine **stopped**. Don't feed it by hand while running.
- Fire risk: balers run hot and dusty — see Module 06.

## Bale wrappers (for haylage)

- The **rotating ring/arms and table move fast and unpredictably** — stay outside the working radius. People have been struck by the wrapping arm.
- Reload film with the machine **stopped**.
- Watch the **drop zone** where the wrapped bale is set down — keep clear, mind toes.
- Stacking wrapped bales: see Module 06.

---

## General machinery good practice

- **Read the operator's manual** for each machine — it's the final word.
- Hitch/unhitch on **firm, level ground**; mind crush points between tractor and machine.
- Don't adjust, grease or unblock with it running.
- Keep bystanders out of the working area entirely.

## Discussion / demonstration

- Point to the PTO guard and anti-rotation chains and explain what they do.
- Recite the blockage-clearing procedure from memory.
- Explain "stored energy" and give an example on a baler/mower.
- Show how you'd connect a PTO shaft safely.

**Sign-off:** Trainee understands PTO/guarding dangers and can correctly clear a blockage (engine off, parts stopped, tool used).
