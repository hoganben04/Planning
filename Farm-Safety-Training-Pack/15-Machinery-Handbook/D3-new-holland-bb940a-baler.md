# D3 — New Holland BB940A (Large Square Baler)

*Read [Module 05 — PTO & field machinery](../05-pto-and-machinery.md) first. This is a **big square
baler** — a large, powerful machine with a pick-up, a plunger that rams at high force, knotters, and
a heavy bale chute. It demands the most care of any field machine here.*

---

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Type | Large (big) square baler |
| Bale size | ~**80 × 90 cm**, length ~1.2–2.5 m — bales are **heavy** (can be several hundred kg) |
| Power needed | Big PTO — needs ~**75 kW / 100 hp+** (run with the Puma 240 CVX) |
| Knotting | Twin/double knotters tie each bale |
| Main hazards | **Plunger** (huge crushing force), **pick-up** entanglement, **knotter** area, **flywheel** momentum, **heavy bale** at the chute, **fire** (hot, dusty), **needles/twine** under tension |

---

## Pre-start daily checks

[A2 daily checks](A2-greasing-and-daily-maintenance.md), plus:

- [ ] **PTO shaft** — full guard + chains, locked both ends.
- [ ] **All guards/covers** on the plunger, knotter, drive and flywheel areas — **none missing**.
- [ ] **Pick-up** tines/teeth condition; no build-up jamming it.
- [ ] **Twine** correctly threaded (do this with the machine **stopped**) and enough on board.
- [ ] **Knotters** clean; needles move freely (per manual).
- [ ] Bale chute/ramp sound; **fire extinguisher** on the tractor/baler; blow off chaff build-up.

## Safe operation

**Hitching**
- Hitch to the Puma on firm, level ground; connect the **big PTO** with the **engine off**; check
  guard and chains; connect hydraulics/electrics for the monitor.

**Starting baling**
- Clear the area of people.
- Engage the PTO at **low revs** — the **flywheel stores huge energy** — then build up to working
  revs. Let the plunger settle into rhythm.
- Drive to keep a steady, even feed; don't overload the pick-up.

**Critical danger zones — keep out while running:**
- **Pick-up** — never reach in; it grabs and pulls.
- **Plunger / bale chamber** — the plunger rams with enough force to **kill instantly**. Never put a
  hand or tool into the chamber while running, and **never** while the flywheel could still turn.
- **Knotter & needles** — under spring tension; **never** poke at a knotter or pull twine while it
  runs.
- **Bale chute** — the **heavy finished bale** moves out under power; **stand clear of the rear**.

**Blockages — the most dangerous moment**
1. **Safe Stop** — PTO disengaged, engine off, **key out and in your pocket**.
2. **Wait for the flywheel and all parts to come to a complete stop** — the flywheel coasts a long
   time.
3. Release any **stored energy / tension** carefully (per manual). Use a tool, not your hands.
4. Replace guards, stand clear, restart at low revs.

> Many baler deaths happen when someone clears a chamber or pick-up with the machine running, or
> before the flywheel stops. **There is no blockage worth your arm or your life.**

**Slopes**
- The baler is **tall and heavy** — go slow, avoid holes and drop-offs, no sharp turns across a slope
  (it can tip). Take care on Sands Hill / The Hill and uneven ground.

**Fire watch**
- Balers run **hot and dusty** — a real fire risk in dry hay. Blow off chaff from hot parts; keep the
  **extinguisher** to hand; carry a phone with the [emergency card](../EMERGENCY-INFORMATION-CARD.md);
  watch for hot bearings / burning smell.

## Greasing & operator maintenance
Safe Stop, key in pocket, **flywheel stopped**. The BB940A has **many grease points** — pick-up,
plunger slides, knotter mechanism, chamber rollers, drive — often grouped on **grease banks** and
some on **auto-lube**. Follow the New Holland schedule and the bank layout your supervisor shows you.
Keep the knotter area clean. Replace all guards before running. See [A2](A2-greasing-and-daily-maintenance.md).

## Shutdown / parking
On firm level ground; lower as designed; Safe Stop; let it cool; never leave it running unattended.

---

## Quick do / don't

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Engage PTO at low revs (flywheel!) | Slam the PTO in at full throttle |
| Stand clear of the rear when a bale ejects | Stand behind during bale discharge |
| Safe Stop + wait for flywheel to STOP before clearing | Reach into pick-up/chamber/knotter running |
| Thread twine with the machine stopped | Feed/pull twine while it runs |
| Keep extinguisher to hand, blow off chaff | Ignore a hot/burning smell |
| Slow on slopes (tall & heavy) | Turn sharply across a slope |
