# E1 — Bale Grabs & Squeeze (Telehandler Attachments)

*Read [Module 04 — Telehandlers](../04-telehandlers-and-loaders.md) and the telehandler pages
([C1](C1-jcb-541-70-telehandler.md), [C2](C2-jcb-516-40-telehandler.md)) first. These are the
attachments that pick up and stack bales — using the **right** one, fitted correctly, is what keeps
bales from rolling back onto the operator.*

---

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Bale grab / grapple | Hydraulic arms that **grip** a bale (or several) from above/around |
| Bale squeeze | Plates that **clamp** bales from the sides |
| Spike (for reference) | A spear into a round bale (Module 06 — never use the plain bucket for bales) |
| Covered by | **LOLER** — lifting attachments must be inspected; **PUWER** — suitable & maintained |
| Main hazards | A **dropped/rolling bale**; the **hydraulic clamp/crush** of the arms; an attachment **not properly locked** on the headstock falling off |

---

## Before use

- [ ] Correct attachment for the bale type (round vs square; grab vs squeeze).
- [ ] **Fitted and locked** onto the telehandler quick-hitch — **pins/locks engaged**, hydraulic
  couplers connected and clean.
- [ ] Arms/plates, pivots and rams sound; **no cracks, no leaks**.
- [ ] **LOLER inspection in date** (your supervisor keeps the record) — a lifting attachment must be
  examined periodically by law.
- [ ] Hydraulic function tested (open/close) before lifting a real bale.

> **Always confirm the quick-hitch is locked.** An attachment that drops off under load is a serious
> falling-object hazard.

---

## Safe use

- Approach the bale square; **grip/clamp firmly** before lifting — make sure it's secure and won't
  slip or roll.
- **Carry low**, boom retracted; travel and turn slowly (telehandler stability — see [C1](C1-jcb-541-70-telehandler.md)).
- **Keep everyone clear** of the load and the machine's path. A bale that slips from a grab/squeeze
  falls fast and **rolls** — it can crush.
- Watch the **load weight** vs the telehandler's chart — wet haylage bales are heavy, and the grab/
  squeeze itself adds weight and changes the load centre.
- Place bales gently and squarely when stacking (Module 06 — bonded, stable, not too high, clear of
  power lines and edges). Round bales **roll** — chock or stack so they can't run away, especially on
  any slope.
- Release only when the bale is **stable and supported** on the stack/ground.

**Crush hazard from the attachment itself**
- The arms/plates close with **hydraulic force** — keep **hands and people out** of the closing area.
- Never reach between the arms/plates or between attachment and bale while the engine is running.

---

## Maintenance
Safe Stop, attachment lowered to the ground, key in pocket. Grease the **arm/plate pivots and ram
pin ends**; check hydraulic hoses/couplers for leaks; check the **quick-hitch locking** mechanism
works. Report any damage and keep the **LOLER inspection** up to date.
See [A2](A2-greasing-and-daily-maintenance.md).

---

## Quick do / don't

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Confirm attachment locked on the hitch | Lift with an unlocked attachment |
| Grip/clamp firmly, check secure before lifting | Lift a loosely held bale |
| Keep people clear of load & rolling path | Let anyone near a suspended bale |
| Mind heavy haylage vs the load chart | Over-reach / overload |
| Keep hands out of the closing arms | Reach between arms/plates while running |
| Keep LOLER inspection in date | Use an out-of-test lifting attachment |
