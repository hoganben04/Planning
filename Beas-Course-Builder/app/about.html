Bea’s Course Builder

Plan show jumping courses and pole work to scale, with every distance worked out for your pony's stride — not a horse's.

The distances, done for you

Tap Check and it walks the course the way an instructor would: how many strides between each pair of fences, and whether that rides true, long or short for the pony you picked.

Why a pony needs different distances distance for n strides = (n + 1) × stride length A horse striding 12ft gives the familiar 24ft one-stride double. A 14.2hh pony striding 3.2m needs 6.4m — nearly a metre shorter. Set her a horse's double and she meets the second element wrong. Most planners assume a horse; this one asks which pony.
  • ✓ Every distance checked against your pony's own canter stride.
  • ✓ Awkward distances flagged. The worst kind is one that falls between two stride numbers — too long for three, too short for four. It says so, and offers to move the fence.
  • ✓ Metres, feet and walking paces. You'll be pacing these out yourself, so "eleven paces" beats "10.4m" when you're stood in the arena.
  • ✓ Course length and time allowed, worked out along the line you'd actually ride, with real turns rather than corners a horse couldn't manage.

Build it to scale

Drop jumps onto a real arena — 20×40, 20×60, 30×60, 40×80, or measure your own — then drag them where you want them. Fences snap onto a true stride distance from the one before, so a good course almost builds itself.

  • ✓ Uprights, oxers, triple bars, walls, planks, gates, Liverpools, water.
  • ✓ Number the fences to set your route. Two a stride apart become a double automatically, lettered A and B.
  • ✓ Pole work too: trotting poles, placing poles and bounces, spaced for your pony's size.
  • ✓ It knows what jumps you own, and tells you when a course needs more wings or poles than you've got.

Take a sheet to the arena

One page with the plan, every fence, every distance in metres, feet and paces, and the time allowed. Print it, or send it to your instructor.

Put it on your phone

There's no App Store listing — it installs straight from Safari, which is why it's free and needs no Apple account.

  1. Open this app in Safari on your iPhone or iPad.
  2. Tap the share button — the square with an arrow coming out of the top.
  3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen, then Add.

Worth doing rather than just bookmarking it. Safari clears a website's saved data after about a week of not visiting, but an app added to the home screen is exempt — so your courses stay put. There's a Save a backup button in Settings as well.

About the heights and distances

Class heights follow British Showjumping and the Pony Club, alongside the unaffiliated heights people actually school over. Check your class schedule before you trust a height: the fence spreads in the app are our own estimates and the speeds are unconfirmed, because the rulebooks couldn't be reached when it was built. Every figure is listed with its source and how much to trust it on the app's Distances screen, along with the things we couldn't establish at all. Walk your distances and check them before you jump.

Everything happens on your own phone. No account, no tracking, nothing uploaded, and it works with no signal.
